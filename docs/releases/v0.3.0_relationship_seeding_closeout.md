# v0.3.0 Relationship Seeding And Known-World Closeout

Epic: `V03-R0-005`

## Scope

This closeout records the accepted `v0.3.0` baseline after the
relationship-seeding and known-world tasks landed across `T01` through
`T05`. It supersedes the earlier audit as the current release-facing
summary of what world init now guarantees, what remains intentionally
bounded, and what later grants, claims, clergy, and office work may rely
on.

## Accepted task sequence

- `T01` established the pre-change audit baseline for world-init
  relationship edges, known houses, and church-adjacent actors.
- `T02` defined the canonical seed-profile schema for liege, clergy, and
  favored or strained local-house baselines.
- `T03` wired non-uniform turn-zero relationship seeding through the
  people-domain relationship engine for player-head edges.
- `T04` promoted known-house relevance from direct blood and marriage ties
  in a deterministic, bounded way.
- `T05` surfaced bounded `known_houses` and `house_dossiers` summaries in
  preview and snapshot surfaces so experience layers can read the social
  baseline directly.

## Locked `v0.3.0` relationship-seeding rules

### Turn-zero relationship seeding is now non-uniform

At world init, the player head no longer starts every seeded social edge
from the same flat baseline:

- liege-facing edges use the canonical liege seed profile
- clergy-facing edges use the canonical church seed profile
- local-house and external-house-head edges use a deterministic favored or
  strained local-house profile

This satisfies the scope-lock requirement that world init starts from
non-uniform seeded relationship edges and scores.

### The differentiation is intentionally limited to world init

The seeded profiles are only applied when turn-zero player-head edges are
first created. Later relationship creation still falls back to the shared
flat baseline. That keeps the released behavior scoped to world
initialization rather than silently changing all future relationship
creation paths.

## Locked known-world baseline assumptions

### T0 and T1 house relevance is deterministic and bounded

The accepted known-world baseline now assumes:

- the player house exists as the T0 anchor
- external houses keep seeded spouse and child graphs from worldgen
- kinship ties imply T1 relevance
- direct blood and marriage ties to the player household can promote a
  house into the bounded relevant-house set even when raw tier inputs would
  not have surfaced it

Promotion and ordering remain deterministic and capped.

### Snapshot and preview surfaces are now canonical inputs

`v0.3.0` preview and bounded-snapshot consumers may rely on:

- `known_houses`
- `house_dossiers`

These surfaces are intentionally coarse. They expose bounded known-house
identity, relationship bands, kinship classes, and household-depth
summaries without requiring UI or reporting code to infer hidden
relationship or heir state.

## Baseline limits that remain intentional

The epic closes with several important limits still in place:

- local noble houses remain shallow single-head shells unless other systems
  later deepen them
- the church still exists as both a clergy person and a minimal parish
  institution rather than a unified institution-holder model
- relationship seeding improves the starting social graph, but grants and
  obligation enforcement do not yet consume that seeded state as a runtime
  decision input

Those are accepted release limits, not regressions in this epic.

## Handoff to grants, claims, clergy, and office work

Follow-on social and fiscal work may now treat the following as locked
inputs:

- liege, clergy, and local-house player-head edges begin from deterministic
  non-uniform seed profiles at world init
- known-house relevance can rise from direct kinship and marriage ties
- preview and bounded snapshots already expose `known_houses` and
  `house_dossiers` as the read surface for relationship-aware experience
  layers

Follow-on work should not assume the following already exist:

- deep local-house family graphs for every local noble shell
- unified clergy-person to parish-institution holder linkage
- grant generation, claims pressure, or office selection that already reads
  seeded relationship strength directly

That leaves the next grants and claims tranche with a stable social
baseline while preserving explicit room for succession, clergy placement,
and office/service mechanics in later backlog scope.
